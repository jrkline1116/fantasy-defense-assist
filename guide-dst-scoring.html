D/ST Scoring Explained: ESPN vs. Yahoo vs. Sleeper

Updated October 14, 2026 · 6 min read

A fantasy defense (D/ST, for defense and special teams) earns points two ways: for big plays, like sacks, takeaways and touchdowns, and for how few points, and sometimes yards, it allows. Every platform scores the big plays about the same. Where they differ is points and yards allowed, and that difference is large enough that the same defense can rank very differently on ESPN than on Yahoo. These are the default settings our grid uses for each tab. Your league may have changed them, so check your league's scoring settings if a number looks off.

Yahoo and Sleeper reward a shutout or near-shutout much more heavily than ESPN does. On those platforms, holding an offense under a touchdown is worth as much as two or three takeaways.

Yards allowed is the biggest difference between the platforms. On ESPN, a defense that gives up 450 yards loses five points even if it forced turnovers, so offenses that move the ball well but stall in the red zone are still dangerous to face. On Yahoo and Sleeper, those same offenses can be fine to stream against, as long as they don't score.

What gets counted against the defense

"Points allowed" usually means points scored against the defense, not the final score. If the opposing defense returns an interception or fumble for a touchdown, that score came off your team's offense, so ESPN and Yahoo don't charge it to your D/ST. A kickoff or punt returned for a touchdown does count, because your special teams allowed it. Our grid follows the same rules.

Why the tabs on our grid disagree

Because the tables differ, the same offense can rank 25th against defenses on ESPN and 15th on Yahoo. An offense that racks up yards but turns the ball over will look friendlier on Yahoo, where yards don't matter. An offense that rarely scores but moves the ball steadily looks friendlier on ESPN. Always use the tab that matches your league. If your league uses custom settings, the closest default tab is still a good guide, and we're working on a custom-scoring option.
