Fantasy Defense Glossary

Updated October 14, 2026 · 4 min read

Short definitions of the terms you'll see on Fantasy Defense Assist and in most fantasy football leagues.

D/ST
Defense and special teams. In fantasy football, an entire NFL team's defense and return units count as one roster spot.
Streaming
Picking up a different defense each week based on its matchup, instead of holding one all season.
Stashing
Picking up a defense before you need it, usually for a run of good matchups or the fantasy playoffs.
Points allowed (PA)
The points the opponent scores against the defense, not counting touchdowns scored by the other defense or its returners off an interception or fumble.
Yards allowed (YA)
Total offensive yards the opponent gains. ESPN scores it by default; Yahoo and Sleeper don't.
Opponent rank vs. D/ST
How an offense ranks by the fantasy points defenses have scored against it. On our grid, 1st is the toughest matchup and 32nd is the easiest.
D/ST rank
How a defense ranks by its own fantasy points this season. 1st has scored the most.
Schedule strength
How easy a defense's remaining games are, based on what its opponents give up to defenses. On our grid, 1st is the easiest.
Fantasy playoffs
The final weeks of a fantasy season, usually NFL Weeks 15, 16 and 17.
Implied team total
The number of points the betting market expects a team to score, based on the game total and point spread.
Pick-six
An interception returned for a touchdown. Worth points for the interception and the touchdown.
Safety
When the offense is tackled in its own end zone. Worth two points for the defense in most formats.
Blocked kick
A blocked punt, field goal or extra point. Usually worth two points for the defense.
Return touchdown
A kickoff or punt returned for a touchdown, credited to the D/ST in most formats.
FAAB
Free Agent Acquisition Budget. A league setting where managers bid on free agents with a season-long budget instead of using waiver order.
Rolling waivers
A waiver system where the manager who makes a claim moves to the back of the order.
Lineup lock
The moment a player can no longer be moved in or out of your lineup, usually when their game kicks off.
Bye week
The week an NFL team doesn't play. Its defense scores zero that week.
