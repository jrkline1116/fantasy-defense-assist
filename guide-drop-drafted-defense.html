When to Drop the Defense You Drafted

Updated October 14, 2026 · 4 min read

A lot of managers draft a defense in the late rounds and then feel committed to it. But where you drafted a defense doesn't change what it will score this week. Here's how to decide whether to keep it.

Compare it to the streaming option

Each week, compare your drafted defense's matchup with the best available one. Open the week's column on the grid, mark the defenses taken in your league, and look at the Best available panel. If a free agent has a much better matchup, starting the streamer is usually right, even if your drafted defense is better on paper.

Keep it if…

  • It ranks near the top in D/ST points and keeps scoring even in average matchups.
  • Its next few matchups are mostly green.
  • Its playoff schedule (Weeks 15–17) is among the easiest.
  • Your bench is deep enough to hold two defenses and pick the better matchup each week.

Drop it if…

  • Its upcoming schedule is mostly red, or its rest-of-season rank is in the bottom third.
  • It's coming off a bye and you need the roster spot.
  • Key defensive players are injured for several weeks.
  • You've been benching it for a streamer most weeks anyway.

The sunk-cost trap

The draft pick is already spent. Holding a defense because it cost you a ninth-round pick doesn't get that pick back. The only question that matters is which defense gives you the most points from here. If you're unsure, check its Rest of season rank on the grid. A defense ranked in the top ten with a top-ten schedule is a keeper. One with a bottom-ten schedule usually isn't.

A middle path

If you're not ready to drop it, bench it in bad weeks and stream instead. That costs a bench spot but keeps your options open. Once its schedule turns, you can cut it with confidence.
