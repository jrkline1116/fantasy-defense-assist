How Our D/ST Matchup Rankings Work

Updated October 14, 2026 · 5 min read

Everything on Fantasy Defense Assist is calculated from public NFL box scores and play-by-play data. We store each game's raw defensive stats and score them in your browser using the tab you pick, so ESPN, Yahoo and Sleeper each get their own numbers. Here's what each number means.

Opponent rank vs. D/STs

For every offense, we take each game it has played, score the defense that faced it, and average those scores. That average is the gray number in each grid cell. Offenses are then ranked from 1st (defenses score the fewest points against them, a tough matchup) to 32nd (defenses score the most, a great matchup). Ties share a rank. This mirrors the opponent rank many fantasy sites show for D/STs.

D/ST rank

Each defense's own rank, shown next to its name, comes from the same stored games: we add up the points it has scored this season in the selected scoring. The most points ranks 1st. Click a team's name to see its week-by-week scoring.

Best available

The Best available panel projects each defense's points for a given week as the average of two numbers: what that defense has scored per game, and what its opponent gives up to D/STs per game. It skips defenses you've marked as taken, games already played and byes. It's a simple, honest projection, not a prediction model. It doesn't know about injuries, weather or a quarterback change, so treat it as a short list, not a final answer.

Rest of season and Playoffs

Schedule strength averages what each defense's remaining opponents give up to D/STs per game, skipping byes and games already played. The Playoffs view does the same for Weeks 15–17 only. 1st means the easiest schedule.

When the numbers update

New results are pulled on Monday, Tuesday and Friday mornings, after the Sunday, Monday night and Thursday night games. Once a game is over, its cell turns gray and shows what each defense scored.

Known limits

  • Small samples early. Through the first few weeks, a single big game can swing an offense's rank a lot.
  • Rare plays. Two-point returns aren't scored, and box-score sack totals occasionally differ by one from a platform's official stats. Our season totals usually match ESPN and Yahoo exactly, and when they don't, the gap is usually a point or two.
  • Default scoring only. If your league changed its D/ST settings, your numbers will differ. Use the closest tab as a guide.
  • Past performance. Ranks describe what has happened this season. Injuries, trades and coaching changes can make an offense better or worse than its numbers.
