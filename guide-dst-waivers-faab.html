D/ST Waiver and FAAB Strategy

Updated October 14, 2026 · 4 min read

Defenses are the most replaceable position in fantasy football. That's what makes streaming work, and it should shape how much you spend to get one.

Rolling waivers

If your league uses rolling waiver priority, don't spend a high claim on a defense in a normal week. Running backs and receivers who earn a bigger role are worth far more. Instead, wait until waivers clear and pick up a defense as a free agent. On most weeks, one of the top few streaming options is still sitting there.

The exception is when two or three managers in your league stream the same way you do. If the best matchup reliably disappears on waivers, put in a claim with your lowest priority, or target next week's matchup a week early.

FAAB (blind bidding)

  • Normal weeks: bid $0 or $1. Most defenses go unclaimed.
  • A clearly great matchup: 1–2% of your starting budget is usually enough.
  • A defense you'll hold for weeks: one with a long run of good matchups or an easy playoff schedule can be worth 3–5%. Use the grid's Playoffs view to find them.

Spending more than that on a defense rarely pays off. That money matters more when a starting running back gets hurt.

Timing your pickup

  • Tuesday or Wednesday: place claims for next week's best matchup.
  • After waivers clear: grab the best remaining free agent if you missed out.
  • Before Thursday night: if your streamer plays on Thursday, make sure it's in your lineup before kickoff. See our Thursday and lineup-lock guide.
  • Sunday morning: check for late quarterback news. A surprise backup starting against a free-agent defense is worth a last-minute swap.

Hold or drop?

After the game, look at your streamer's next two weeks on the grid. If both are green, hold it and save a move. If next week is red, drop it and stream again.
