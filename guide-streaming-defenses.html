How to Stream Fantasy Defenses Every Week

Updated October 14, 2026 · 5 min read

Streaming a defense means you don't hold one D/ST all season. Instead, each week you pick up whichever available defense has the best matchup, play it, and drop it for the next one. Done well, a streamed defense routinely outscores the "set it and forget it" defense that went in the middle rounds of your draft. Here's a routine that takes about five minutes a week.

Why streaming works

Defensive fantasy points are driven more by the opponent than by the defense itself. Sacks, interceptions and fumbles happen when a quarterback holds the ball too long or an offense makes mistakes, and points-allowed bonuses depend on how good that offense is at scoring. An average defense facing a turnover-prone offense with a weak line is often a better start than a great defense facing an elite offense. That's why most of the information on our grid is about the opponent, not the defense.

The weekly routine

  1. Tuesday or Wednesday, before waivers run: open the grid and click the header of next week's column. That sorts every defense by how many fantasy points D/STs have scored against its opponent this season, best matchups on top.
  2. Mark the taken defenses. Flip the switch beside every D/ST already rostered in your league, then turn on Hide taken. What's left is your real menu.
  3. Check the Best available panel. It blends each remaining defense's own scoring with how friendly its opponent is, so a decent defense with a great matchup rises above a great defense with a tough one.
  4. Look one week past this one. If two options are close, take the one whose following week is also green. Holding a defense for two straight good matchups saves you a waiver move.
  5. Check the news before kickoff. A backup quarterback starting against your defense is good news; a weather delay or a returning star quarterback can change the picture.

Green, red and what's in between

Each matchup cell shows the opponent, its rank against D/STs, and the average points defenses have scored against it. 21st–32nd (green) means defenses have done well against that offense: stream it. 1st–10th (red) means defenses have struggled: avoid it unless you have no choice. The middle of the pack is fine when nothing better is available. Early in the season, treat the colors with some caution. Three or four games is a small sample, and one fluky pick-six can move an offense several spots.

When to pick up, and when to hold

Waiver priority matters. If your league uses rolling waivers, a defense rarely deserves a top claim, so use free agency after waivers clear when you can. If your league uses a budget (FAAB), a dollar or two is usually plenty. The exceptions are a defense with a run of three or more good matchups, or one with a great fantasy playoff schedule. Those are worth holding instead of streaming. Our playoff planning guide covers that second case.

Common mistakes

  • Chasing last week's points. A defense that scored 20 last week had a matchup; the question is what this week's opponent looks like.
  • Ignoring byes. Bye weeks are marked on the grid. Don't pick up a defense the week before its bye unless you plan to drop it right away.
  • Forgetting your scoring. ESPN scores yards allowed and Yahoo doesn't, which changes which matchups matter. Pick your platform's tab, and read how each one scores defenses if you're not sure.
  • Starting a defense against an elite offense out of loyalty. Benching a big-name defense in a bad matchup is often the right call.
