Thursday Night Defenses and Lineup Locks

Updated October 14, 2026 · 4 min read

Most fantasy platforms lock each player when their game kicks off. For defenses, that means Thursday night and Monday night games need a little planning.

How lineup locks work

On most platforms, including ESPN, Yahoo and Sleeper by default, a player locks individually at the start of their own game. You can still change anyone who hasn't played yet. Some leagues use a weekly lock instead, where every lineup locks at the first game of the week. Check your league's settings so you know which one you're dealing with.

Thursday night streamers

  • Decide early. If the best matchup of the week plays on Thursday, you need to pick it up and start it before Thursday kickoff. Waivers that process Wednesday night usually give you time; free-agent pickups on Thursday afternoon work too.
  • No take-backs. Once the Thursday defense plays, it's locked in. If it has a bad night, you can't swap to a Sunday defense.
  • Short weeks cut both ways. Both teams are on short rest. That tends to keep scoring down, which can help defenses, but it isn't a strong enough effect to override a bad matchup.

Monday night as a fallback

Late games give you flexibility. If you're deciding between two similar defenses, starting the one that plays later lets you react to injury news and Sunday results. If your Sunday defense ends up inactive or the weather turns, a Monday night defense on the waiver wire can still be picked up.

What the grid shows

Once a game has been played, its cell on the grid turns gray and shows what that defense scored. Those defenses are left out of the Best available suggestions for that week. After the Sunday games, the suggestions move on to next week, since that's what most people are planning for.
